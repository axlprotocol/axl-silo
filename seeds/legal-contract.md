SITUATION:
TechCorp (plaintiff) contracted CloudBase (defendant) for a $47M enterprise cloud migration. The project ran 14 months past deadline. TechCorp claims $12M in damages from business disruption. CloudBase claims TechCorp changed scope 23 times and owes $8M in change orders. Both sides have counsel. Discovery is underway.

KEY FACTS:
- Original contract: $47M, 18-month delivery, fixed price with change order provisions
- Actual delivery: 32 months (14 months late)
- Change orders submitted by CloudBase: 23 (TechCorp approved 9, rejected 14)
- TechCorp's internal emails show they knew scope was expanding but did not sign change orders
- CloudBase's PM was replaced 3 times during the project
- SLA violations: 47 documented, CloudBase acknowledges 31
- Data breach during migration: 12,000 customer records exposed. Remediation cost: $2.1M.
- Insurance: CloudBase has $10M E&O policy. TechCorp has $5M cyber policy.

1. @Plaintiff_Counsel — TechCorp's lawyer. Aggressive. Wants maximum damages.
2. @Defense_Counsel — CloudBase's lawyer. Focused on change order defense and comparative fault.
3. @Mediator — Neutral. Evaluates settlement range.
4. @Judge — Trial judge (if it goes to litigation). Known for strict contract interpretation.
5. @Insurance_Adjuster — Evaluating coverage for both policies.
6. @Data_Privacy_Expert — Assessing the breach liability separately from the contract dispute.

THE QUESTION:
Given the facts, which resolution path optimizes outcomes for both parties?
A: Full litigation (jury trial, 6 months away)
B: Binding arbitration (faster, private, limited discovery)
C: Mediation with structured settlement framework
